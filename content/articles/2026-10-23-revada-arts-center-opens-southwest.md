A new performing arts building opens in Southwest Washington this week. Three local groups will share it: a theater company, an opera company and an independent cinema. The Revada Arts Center, at 340 Maple Drive SW, holds a week of launch events from Friday, Oct. 23, through Tuesday, Oct. 27. The schedule is posted on the [center's website](https://www.revadaartscenter.org/).

## Who is moving in

Theater Alliance is the lead organization. It holds the lease and takes on most of the financial risk, according to [The 51st](https://51st.news/southwest-dc-revada-arts-center-theater-alliance/) and [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2026/10/21/new-dc-arts-hub-is-envisioned-haven-theater-opera-cinema/). Its partners are IN Series, an opera company that has never had a permanent home, and 4EYE Film Center, a new art house cinema led by founder Emily Lenzner. The Post reports that all three have long-term agreements; The 51st reports that Theater Alliance signed a 15-year lease and that the partners will share governance, programming plans and some expenses.

Theater Alliance already knows the address. The company had been running a temporary residency there before the build-out, and its [website](https://theateralliance.org/) still lists 340 Maple Drive SW as its home.

## What's inside

Both outlets put the building at about 9,000 square feet. The main room is a 140-seat black box theater with flexible seating and staging, which Theater Alliance and IN Series will use as their primary stage. The building also has a 58-seat cinema for 4EYE's screenings, a rehearsal hall, and co-working and community space. The center's website also lists a micro-proscenium stage, a scene shop, a costume shop and a green room.

The 51st reports that a community access program will let other local groups book the space for their own shows, rehearsals and gatherings.

## Launch schedule

According to the center's published schedule:

- **Friday, Oct. 23:** An opening ceremony with a ribbon cutting and a discussion about shared arts space. The Post describes this event as invitation-only.
- **Saturday, Oct. 24:** A community open house with family programs, music classes, acting workshops and tours of the building.
- **Monday, Oct. 26:** A co-working session for performing artists and community organizations, with free headshots.
- **Tuesday, Oct. 27:** A screening of the 2025 film *Blue Moon*, presented by 4EYE Film Center and IN Series. A live concert of Rodgers and Hart songs follows.

## Why it matters locally

The project's backers describe it as a new model for small arts groups, which struggle to pay for permanent space on their own. A shared building lets them split the cost. Shanara Gabrielle, Theater Alliance's executive artistic director, told The 51st that the partners see their success as linked: "A win for one is a win for all."

Ward 6 Councilmember Charles Allen, whose ward includes the Southwest Waterfront, issued a statement backing the project, The 51st reported.

## What's next

After launch week, 4EYE plans to hold regular film screenings and Theater Alliance will begin its season in the black box, according to the Post. Theater Alliance's 2026-27 lineup, listed on its website, includes *Cold War Choir Practice*, *Fool for Love* and *Business Ideas*.
