Companies that rent dockless scooters and e-bikes in the District can now be fined when their devices end up blocking sidewalks or bike lanes, under final regulations the [District Department of Transportation announced Friday, Oct. 23](https://ddot.dc.gov/release/ddot-announces-new-regulations-strengthen-shared-micromobility-safety-accessibility-and).

The rules arrive during a period of heightened attention to shared scooters and e-bikes, following a fatal scooter collision earlier this month and a police safety campaign that began Oct. 16.

## What the new rules do

According to DDOT, operators can be fined for devices that are parked improperly, for not moving badly parked devices quickly enough, for blocking pedestrian routes and bike lanes, and for failing to meet the agency's fleet deployment standards. The maximum fine is $100. The agency said the goal is to make companies take more responsibility for how their fleets are managed on city streets.

The regulations also overhaul the low-income discount that operators must offer. Eligible riders now get a monthly credit of 300 minutes, which does not roll over, and a 50% discount once those minutes are used. DDOT said the structure mirrors the low-income program for Capital Bikeshare and that operators have begun moving enrolled riders to it.

"Shared micromobility is a fast-growing segment of the District's transportation network," DDOT Director Sharon Kershbaum said in the release.

## More rules still to come

DDOT said it will issue additional regulations in the coming months in response to public comments on a proposal it published Sept. 4. Those rules are expected to address vehicle speeds and sidewalk riding, the permit application process and permit terms, and the performance standards companies must meet before adding vehicles.

[WJLA reported](https://wjla.com/news/local/dc-scooter-safety-ddot-road-speed-rental-electric-sidewalk-department-of-transportation-8th-street-pennsylvania-avenue-southeast-k-north-capitol) that the September proposal would raise the street speed limit for rental scooters from 10 mph to 15 mph while keeping lower speeds on sidewalks. Ralph Buehler, a Virginia Tech professor who studies transportation, described the change to the station as a trade-off between keeping pace with traffic and the severity of crashes.

## Police campaign and a fatal crash

The Metropolitan Police Department launched a citywide education effort on Oct. 16 aimed at e-bike and e-scooter riders, which is scheduled to run through the end of the year. Officers are focusing on areas where the devices are heavily used, including the U Street corridor, Navy Yard and Gallery Place-Chinatown, according to [WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/local/dc-police-ramp-up-ebike-scooter-safety-efforts-amid-growing-concerns-traffic-fatalities-crashes-talib-shakir-eastern-market) and [FOX 5 DC](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/dc-police-launch-safety-crackdown-e-bikes-e-scooters-amid-increase-deaths).

Police say traffic deaths in D.C. are up 62% this year, both outlets reported; WJLA said the comparison is with the same period in 2025. The campaign followed the Oct. 9 death of Talib Shakir, 50, director of the Mayor's Office on Returning Citizen Affairs, after two electric scooters collided near Eastern Market. Police said the crash remains under investigation.

Police are reminding riders to travel with the flow of traffic, yield to pedestrians, obey signals, stay off sidewalks in the Central Business District and wear helmets. WJLA reported that police have not directly tied the campaign to Shakir's death, or to recent robberies in which, according to a station investigation, suspects fled on rental scooters or e-bikes.

## What's next

DDOT has not announced a date for the next set of rules on speeds and sidewalk riding. Information on the program and permits is posted on the agency's [shared mobility site](https://sharedmobility.ddot.dc.gov). The MPD outreach campaign is scheduled to continue through Dec. 31.
