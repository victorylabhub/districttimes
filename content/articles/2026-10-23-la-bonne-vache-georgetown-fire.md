A fire Sunday night, Oct. 18, heavily damaged the first floor of La Bonne Vache, a French bistro in Georgetown. Two people who live above the restaurant were forced out of their apartment. The owners say they plan to rebuild.

D.C. Fire and EMS crews were called to the 3200 block of Prospect Street NW at about 9:30 p.m., according to [Washingtonian](https://washingtonian.com/2026/10/19/la-bonne-vache-in-georgetown-devastated-by-fire/) and [FOX 5 DC](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/fire-tears-through-popular-georgetown-restaurant-la-bonne-vache-displacing-2-residents). Washingtonian reported the department's account: the call came in at 9:34 p.m., and firefighters were on scene within two minutes.

## What happened

Fire officials said the fire started at an electrical panel on the restaurant's first floor, both outlets reported; Washingtonian said the exact cause remained under investigation. FOX 5 said employees were getting ready to close for the night when it broke out. Crews kept the fire to the first floor of the three-story building. Photos released by officials show scorched walls, shattered windows and overturned furniture throughout the dining room. The front windows have since been boarded up.

No injuries were reported. Staff got out, and so did the two adults who live upstairs. Both were displaced. Washingtonian reported that the residents are getting help from the Mayor's Office of Community Relations and Services and the American Red Cross, and that Pepco cut utility service to the building.

## A neighborhood favorite

La Bonne Vache opened in January 2024 at the corner of Prospect and Potomac streets, in the space long occupied by the Booeymonger deli. That was reported at the time by [Washingtonian](https://washingtonian.com/2024/01/25/an-approachable-affordable-french-bistro-finally-arrives-in-georgetown/) and [The Georgetowner](https://georgetowner.com/?p=270781). Claire and Ari Wilder run the bistro, and Rachel and Robert Aikens are co-owners.

Neighbors told FOX 5 the restaurant was usually full, with waits that could reach 20 minutes. They said they were relieved that everyone got out safely.

## What's next

The owners were not available for interviews. In a social media post, they thanked the community and said they would reopen. Washingtonian quoted the post: "we will rebuild and reopen." Ari Wilder told Washingtonian the team's first priority is its employees.

No timeline for repairs or reopening has been announced. The restaurant is still closed.
